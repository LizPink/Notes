# Prompt
Technological advances have had more neagative impacts on human interaction and relationships than positive ones.

# Essay
The past twenty years have engendered more connectivity than all previous millennia of our species's existence. With so much change in such short period of time, determining whether this phenomenon has been largely positive or negative is difficult.  The prompt takes a negative stance and argues that technological advancements have resulted in more harm than good. In my opinion, I mostly disgress with this view and believe that technological progression has improved our connectivity and relationships for the following two reasons, though I do concede that recent advancements have unfortunatly decrease our need for face-to-face connection.

First of all, technological advancements have resulted in more benefits for our intetconnectivity because, as a species, we are no longer constrained by distance. For example, a Japanese exchange student studying in New York can esaily and effectively communicate with his mother with no charge using an application like WhatsApp and Facebook. In the past, this same student would have no choice but to send a letter (which might arrives three weeks later) or call long distance at an exorbitant rate dollars per minute. The Earth is massive, and even one travels at the maximun speed of a commercial jetliner, it would take nearly 24 hours from one end of the planet to the other. However, technology, at least from a communication point of view, has created a reality in which all Earth's 8 billion inhabitants are effectively right next to each other, not constrained by distance in any meaningful way. The advantages of this leap cannot be overstated, from the way we communicate with our families to the way we conduct business around the globe.

Second of all, the progression of our technology has allowed not just for ability to sustain our relationships but also the capacity to form new ones and become part of distinct communities. For example, an individual from a small town in America might be partial to the role-playing game Dungeon and Dragon and be able to cultivate this hobby by joining a local league or in a larger town he discovered on the Internet. Thirty years ago, this individual would have found challenging to engage with this hobby and perhaps would never have persued it given the difficulty of finding like-minded peers. Humans have an inherent need to be part of a tribe, to be part of a group that shares their interests and passions. Technology makes this possible, particularly for those individuals with niche hobbies that are not necessarily shared by those closest to them. Such freedom of expression has led to millions of people thriving in life and finding their true callings.

However, I do concede that has led to the unfortunate consequence that people no longer feeling the need to have face-to-face interation. For example, especially after the Covid-19 pandemic, millions of workers of the globe no longer deem it necessary to go to their office and meet their co-workers on a personal level. Instead, they can simply use their video conferencing apps like Zoom or Google Meet. In addition, the phenomenon of "video dating" is becoming increasingly popular, where a date is conducted entirely online rather than at a coffee shop or restaurant. As referenced above, humans are extremly social animals. We have instintual need to be around other humans. Multiple experiments have showned that if a person is deprived of contact with other members of the species, a host of physical and mental consequences result. Teconology, in some way, is undermining this evolutionary need, one that has been with us for hundreds of thousands of years. It is possible that we  adjust to the new paradigm, but it is doubtful that this adaptation occurs at the same rate of technological progession. It is no surprising that we are seeing rising depression and anxiety levels around the world, even as our standards of life improve.

In conclusion, while it is true that technological eroded some of our conncectivity, particularly our face-to-face interactio, the benefits of instant communication unrestricted by distance and forming connections with like-minded people clearly outweigh the cons.
